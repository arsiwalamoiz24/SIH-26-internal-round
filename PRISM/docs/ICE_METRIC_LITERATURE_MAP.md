# ICE_METRIC_LITERATURE_MAP — every candidate ice-evidence metric, audited

**Date:** 2026-08-26. Built before writing any V2 code, per task instruction.
Every metric PRISM currently uses, plus every metric proposed for V2, is
recorded here with its physical meaning, literature source, ice-evidence
status, known false positives, and a confidence rating. **A metric is not
retained merely because PRISM v1 already uses it.**

**Epistemic key** (as in the three prior reports): **FACT** = verified
against a primary/full-text source. **OBSERVATION** = a pattern noticed in
FACTs. **HYPOTHESIS** = untested/partially-tested explanation.
Sources marked "search-summary" were not independently fetched in full text
— treat as moderate-, not high-, confidence.

| Metric | Physical meaning | Literature source | Evidence for ice | Known false positives | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **CPR (raw, CPR>1 heuristic)** | Ratio of same-sense to opposite-sense circularly polarized backscatter (or the linear-pol proxy μc PRISM/Sinha use); classically interpreted as a proxy for multiple/volume scattering | Historically: Campbell et al. (radar polarimetry of rough terrain); Spudis et al. 2013 (pro-ice, JGR); **Neish et al. (2011), *JGR Planets* 116, E01005, DOI 10.1029/2010JE003647 — full text obtained, CONFIRMED**: only 2% of Mini-RF pixels and 0.01% of Chandrayaan-1 Mini-SAR pixels at Cabeus have CPR>1; Cabeus's mean CPR (0.25±0.12) is *below* the south-polar regional average (0.31±0.17) | Weak-to-moderate, contested — **and directly falsified as a *necessary* condition by Neish et al. 2011's own confirmed-positive-control site (Cabeus)** | **Fresh-crater ejecta, block fields, impact melt, crater-wall steepness** — extensively documented (Eke et al. 2014, Fa 2018); Verma et al. 2025 reportedly attributes CPR>1 "primarily to surface roughness" in several craters (search-summary confidence only — ScienceDirect fully inaccessible in two independent attempts, **the specific R²~0.99 CPR-DOP inverse-correlation figure attributed to this paper is UNVERIFIED and must not be cited as fact**) | **LOW as a standalone ice indicator** — retained only as a relative/contextual signal, never as a binary threshold. Neish et al. 2011's own interpretation: low CPR rules out a *thick, near-surface* ice deposit, not ice mixed as fine grains (<~10cm) into regolith — i.e., CPR is not just weak evidence, it can be **actively misleading in either direction** |
| **CPR as a local relative anomaly** (interior−exterior delta, same acquisition/calibration) | Same physical basis as raw CPR, but isolates a candidate's radar response from its own immediate surroundings, controlling for regional/acquisition-level effects | No single named published method found for this *exact* construction; it is methodologically analogous to background-subtraction approaches used generically in remote sensing anomaly detection | Moderate — removes some regional confounds, does not resolve the ice-vs-roughness ambiguity | Roughness that is itself spatially anomalous within the same window (a rough PSR floor amid smoother surroundings) will still register | MODERATE — a real improvement over the raw threshold, not a fix for the underlying ambiguity |
| **DOP (PRISM's linear-pol Stokes construction)** | Attempts to quantify how "polarized" the backscattered field is; low DOP interpreted (by Sinha 2026, citing Raney-style hybrid-pol formalism) as volume-scattering-dominated | Sinha et al. 2026 (formula stated, channel mapping unstated); Raney et al. 2012 (m-χ, genuinely a 2-channel hybrid-pol construction); PRISM's own `DOP_SINHA_2026_RESEARCH.md` (8 independent hypotheses, all returning 0.63–0.86 vs. Sinha's claimed 0.10–0.13) | **UNRESOLVED — do not use as ground truth** | Basis mismatch (quad-pol HH/VV pairing is non-standard per general SAR pedagogy, `LUNAR_SOUTH_POLE_ICE_VALIDATION_LITERATURE.md` §16), calibration/crosstalk (tested, largely ruled out), acquisition/processing differences vs. Sinha (unresolved) | **NONE, pending resolution** — moved to `DOP_DIAGNOSTIC`, excluded from the primary score |
| **Stokes parameters (S1–S4), general** | Standard formalism describing partial polarization of a coherent wave | Textbook (classical optics DOP formalism); confirmed standard *shape*, not a lunar-specific citation | N/A — a mathematical tool, not itself evidence | Only as valid as the physical channels fed into it (see DOP row) | N/A |
| **Depolarization / cross-pol power** | Fraction of backscattered energy appearing in the cross-polarized channel; associated with multiple-bounce/volume scattering | General SAR literature (search-summary, `ice_v2_literature_verification.md` §E) confirms this is a real, standard physical quantity | Weak-to-moderate — same ambiguity as volume scattering generally (buried rocks, layering, and ice all produce it) | Rock fields, layered regolith, any subsurface heterogeneity | LOW-MODERATE as an ice-specific signal |
| **Pv (Yamaguchi 4-component volume-scattering fraction)** | `Pv = vol/(evn+vol+odd+hlx)` — fraction of total backscattered power attributed to the "volume scattering" component of a standard polarimetric decomposition | Yamaguchi decomposition is a well-established, widely-cited general SAR technique (not lunar/ice-specific) | Weak — same volume-scattering ambiguity as depolarization above; not validated as ice-specific by any source found in this investigation | Rough/blocky regolith, buried rock fields, any subsurface structural heterogeneity | **LOW as a standalone ice indicator** — real, correctly-computed, but its ice-specificity is unestablished in the literature |
| **SERD** | ISRO/DFSAR-internal "Single-bounce Eigenvalue Relative Difference," described by PRISM's own docs as a roughness indicator | **No independent literature validating this as an ice indicator was found in either this or the prior investigation.** ISRO's own algorithm is not documented in the CH2DFSAR SIS/user manual (`SERD_NAN_ANALYSIS.md`, already established) | **UNKNOWN — no external literature basis found at all** | Unknown (algorithm undocumented) | **NONE** — genuinely experimental, PRISM-internal only |
| **T-Ratio** | ISRO/DFSAR-internal quantity, PRISM's docs describe as a dielectric-constant proxy | **Same as SERD — no independent external literature citation was found for this specific DFSAR-derived product** | UNKNOWN | Unknown | **NONE** — genuinely experimental, PRISM-internal only |
| **Radar cross section (RCS) / absolute backscatter power** | Total returned power per unit area, before any ratio/polarimetric decomposition | Standard radar physics; underlies every derived metric above | N/A — a raw physical quantity, not itself an ice test | Surface material, roughness, incidence angle all affect it | N/A (a building block, not a metric to score) |
| **Incidence-angle-normalized CPR** | Corrects CPR for the fact that backscatter (and CPR) varies systematically with radar look/incidence angle across a scene | **No single lunar-CPR-specific standard exists.** General SAR/planetary-radar literature uses a family of empirical cosine-power-law normalizations (e.g. Mladenova et al. 2013, IEEE TGRS, "Incidence Angle Normalization of Radar Backscatter Data" — exact equation not independently retrieved); Earth-based lunar radar (Arecibo-GBT) reportedly normalizes to a cos⁴ law (search-summary, moderate confidence). Any correction PRISM adopts must be labeled "adapted from the general cosine-power-law family, not a lunar-CPR canonical method" | Would improve CPR's reliability if applied, in principle | Does not resolve the ice-vs-roughness ambiguity by itself | **NOT COMPUTABLE in PRISM's current data regardless of method choice** — no per-pixel incidence-angle raster exists anywhere in PRISM's Y4R/L3C mosaic products (confirmed this session by inspecting `radar_pipeline.py`/`candidate_physics_pipeline.py` — only whole-scene scalar incidence angles exist in individual raw-acquisition XML labels, not spatially resolved for the mosaic) |
| **Surface roughness (LOLA DEM-derived RMS/TRI)** | Real, independently-measured small-scale topographic roughness from laser altimetry | Riley, Degloria & Elliott (1999) for TRI; standard photogrammetric roughness metrics generally | Not an ice indicator on its own — used here as the confound CPR/Pv must be checked against | N/A (it IS the confound, not a thing that has its own false positives in this context) | **HIGH as a terrain-roughness measurement** (real, independently verified, matches NASA's own precomputed slope product to within 0.01° per `DECISIONS.md`) — **not an ice indicator** |
| **Slope** | Real DEM-derived terrain gradient | Standard photogrammetry | Not an ice indicator — hazard/traversability relevant only | N/A | HIGH as a terrain measurement, not ice-relevant |
| **Temperature (Diviner)** | Real measured/modeled surface/subsurface temperature | Paige et al. 2010; Vasavada, Paige & Wood 1999 (110 K ice-stability threshold, not independently re-verified — `LUNAR_SOUTH_POLE_ICE_VALIDATION_LITERATURE.md` §9) | Necessary-condition plausibility only — cold enough for ice to survive is not evidence ice is present | N/A | MODERATE-HIGH for plausibility, **zero for detection** |
| **PSR status (permanent shadow)** | Necessary geometric/thermal precondition for long-term ice stability | Watson, Murray & Brown 1961 (foundational cold-trap concept) | Necessary-condition plausibility only | N/A | HIGH as a precondition, **zero as detection** — PRISM's own illumination=0.0 confirmation for all 7 candidates is real and independently geometric, not radar-derived |
| **ShadowCam reflectance/radiance contrast** | Optical brightness anomaly at visible wavelengths, tested against M3 ice-detection pixels | Ando, Li, Robinson & Wagner (2025), *Planetary Science Journal* 6(3):62 — full text obtained | **Confirmed methodology, but a mostly-null result at the south pole** — only Cabeus (of 14 tested PSRs) shows the expected M3-consistent shift | Requires ~20–30 wt% ice for visible-wavelength contrast — real ice below this concentration would be invisible to this test (a false *negative* risk, not a false positive) | MODERATE — a real, validated method, but its south-polar track record is mostly negative even at confirmed-M3 sites |
| **ShadowCam "forward scattering"** | Water-ice/regolith mixtures are physically more forward-scattering than pure regolith, increasingly so with ice content — a real physical property | **CONFIRMED NOT YET AN OPERATIONALIZED METHOD.** Ando et al. 2025's actual, published method is single-phase-angle (fixed nadir geometry) maximum-radiance comparison, **not** a forward-scattering/phase-function test — they discuss forward scattering explicitly only as **future work**, citing a separate, not-independently-verified paper (Li et al. 2024) | The underlying physics is real and citable | N/A — not yet a testable method | **NONE — do not implement as if it were an established criterion.** Treating an unpublished future-work idea as an existing PRISM-usable threshold would itself be a fabrication; V2 must use only the radiance-contrast method Ando et al. 2025 actually validated |
| **M3 spectral ice evidence** | Direct 1.3/1.5/2.0 μm water-ice absorption features, spectral-angle-mapped against lab standards | Li et al. 2018, *PNAS* — full text obtained | **Direct evidence where present**; explicit non-detection is meaningful (not merely "unstudied") where checked and absent | Detection is at ~280 m/px and requires the specific spectral signature; **absence of detection is not proof of absence** below M3's sensitivity or outside its exact examined pixels | **HIGH where present or explicitly checked-negative; NOT AVAILABLE for any of PRISM's own 7 candidates** (confirmed, `LUNAR_SOUTH_POLE_ICE_VALIDATION_LITERATURE.md`) |
| **LEND hydrogen enhancement** | Regional neutron-suppression-derived hydrogen abundance | Sanin et al. 2017, *Icarus* | Real, but **10 km FWHM resolution — coarser than any single PRISM candidate (9–43 km²)** | Cannot be candidate-specific by construction | MODERATE for regional context, **essentially zero for candidate-level discrimination** |
| **LCROSS direct water detection** | Direct in-situ spectroscopic measurement of an impact-excavated ejecta plume | Colaprete et al. 2010, *Science* | **The single highest-confidence ice detection available** | A single point measurement, not a spatial map; cannot be generalized to other PSRs without independent evidence | **HIGHEST — but exists for exactly one location (Cabeus) in this entire investigation, and Cabeus is not among PRISM's candidates** |
| **Isolation Forest anomaly score (v1, PSR-level)** | Unsupervised outlier detection over 336 PSRs, using features derived from the same Pv computation used to rank the candidates | PRISM's own `ML_METHODS.md` already documents this as circular (self-acknowledged, not this investigation's finding) | **None independent of Pv's own (unestablished) ice-specificity** | Same as Pv | **NONE as independent validation** — explicitly circular by PRISM's own admission |
| **Isolation Forest anomaly score (v2, pixel-level)** | Per-pixel outlier detection using 4 independently-measured bands (Pv, CPR, SERD, T-Ratio) | Same caveat as each input metric individually (Pv/CPR weak, SERD/T-Ratio unvalidated) | Only as strong as its weakest independent-but-unvalidated inputs | Same as each input metric | **LOW-MODERATE** — methodologically non-circular, but built on metrics with their own unresolved ice-specificity |

## Metrics recommended for `EXPERIMENTAL_METRICS` (removed from the primary V2 score)

Per task instruction §6: "if a metric has no defensible literature basis for
ice detection, move it to `EXPERIMENTAL_METRICS`."

- **SERD** — no independent literature basis found at all (not merely
  contested, genuinely absent).
- **T-Ratio** — same.
- **DOP (PRISM's current construction)** — unresolved vs. the one published
  comparison point (Sinha 2026); moved to a separate `DOP_DIAGNOSTIC` field
  per explicit task instruction, not deleted.
- **Isolation Forest (both versions)** — inherits, not adds to, the
  ice-specificity of its underlying features; kept as a reported diagnostic,
  not a primary score input.

## Metrics retained in the primary V2 score, with explicit caveats

- **CPR, as a local relative anomaly** (interior−exterior delta within the
  same acquisition), not a raw CPR>1 threshold.
- **Pv, as a local relative anomaly**, same construction — genuinely
  measures volume scattering, but explicitly flagged as *not* ice-specific
  on its own; used only as a supporting "Radar Evidence" component, never as
  independent confirmation.
- **Terrain roughness/slope** — not scored as ice evidence at all; used
  exclusively as the confound-check for the CPR/Pv anomalies (§4 of the
  redesign), and separately as hazard/traversability output (kept fully
  apart from ice evidence, per task instruction §12).
- **Illumination / PSR status** — real, geometrically-confirmed, used as
  Level-C physical-plausibility evidence only, never conflated with
  detection.
